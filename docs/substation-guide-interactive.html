The Substation
Guide

How a substation is modelled in VoltEdge, and how to model one yourself. Two books braided into one: the step-by-step path for building a station in the app, and the reference for why the model is shaped the way it is.

A substation is not one object. It is a place plus a population of equipment, tied together by relationships. The equipment, not the boundary, is where the electrical reality lives.

01

What a substation is in VoltEdge

The mental model: one place, one population, two graphs.

The trap of the word “substation”

Kwame has designed substations for fifteen years. He types “substation” into the class list expecting the substation object: rating, voltage, fence, bays, schematic. He places a SUBSTATION and gets a polygon. No transformers inside, no switchgear, no voltage. A coloured boundary on the map.

That polygon is the place. The transformers, busbars, switches, panels and batteries are separate features, each its own class with its own identity, that happen to live inside the polygon and wire together electrically.

∑ Why not one fat object?

A transformer is a network node; a fence is not

Load flow walks through a transformer (HV in, LV out, impedance drop, tap ratio). It never walks through a boundary. Plant as features with terminals lets the analyses treat the station as what it is: a small network.

Equipment has independent lifecycles

A breaker is replaced, a transformer uprated, a battery bank swapped. One record cannot carry twenty independent condition ratings and histories.

The same classes appear outside substations

POWER_XFMR, MV_BUSBAR and switches also exist at pole tops, packaged substations and customer yards. One definition, one symbology, one rule set everywhere.

Metadata, not code

A class is rows in metadata.feature_classes / fields / terminals / network_rules. Adding the station-service transformer was a seed file, not a migration and redeploy.

The two graphs

Every piece of equipment sits in two completely separate graphs. Confusing them is the number-one modelling mistake. Hover any item below and watch where it appears in each graph, and where it is missing.

▶ Rule of thumb at the keyboard

Dragging a feature into a station does not wire it to anything. These are three separate acts:

Placementsets geometry
≠
Containmenta relationship you or an assembly create
≠
Connectivitywiring at the terminals

If a trace can’t find a transformer you just placed, you have geometry without connectivity. That is the model asking you to wire it.

02

The yard

Primary plant. Every class here is a live junction with terminals.

What “a junction with terminals” means

The power transformer — POWER_XFMR

The heart of the yard. Adenta has two, T1A and T1B, both two-winding.

▶ Placing a power transformer
  1. Inside the SUBSTATION polygon, choose Power Transformer and place a point where T1A stands.
  2. Choose the subtype: two_winding for a plain 33/11 kV unit.
  3. Fill the nameplate. primary_voltage (33KV) and condition_rating are required; everything else is optional so an un-surveyed unit degrades gracefully.
∑ How it behaves in a load flow, for free
FieldWhat the sweep does with it
primary_voltage (coded, e.g. 33KV)HV nominal, the “from” voltage
secondary_voltage (numeric, e.g. 11000)LV nominal. Both present ⇒ voltage-transforming node; absent ⇒ pass-through
impedance_pct + x_r_rationameplate %Z and X/R: the series impedance drop
rated_mvaloading % is reported against it
tap_position + tap_step_pcteffective LV ratio × 1 + position·step/100, so LV voltage tracks the live tap
vector_grouprecord-only until a meshed/parallel study exists

Tertiary fields (tertiary_voltage, z_hv_tv_pct, …) are recorded and staged; the sweep models HV↔LV today. Record the field first, key an engine off it later is a recurring VoltEdge discipline. Map symbol: a teal square, distinct from the blue DIST_XFMR.

The busbar — MV_BUSBAR

A solid equipotential conductor, not a set of switchable ways. Two terminals scale to any number of feeders: T1 is the incoming way, T2 is the bar that fans out, and every outgoing circuit is a distinct connectivity edge on the same T2.

▶ Placing Adenta’s two busbars

Place one MV_BUSBAR for 33 kV and a second for 11 kV, each with its voltage set. They are not connected to each other directly; the transformers sit between them. Two features (not one) is what lets the model say “33 kV live, 11 kV dead” during a transformer outage.

The earthing transformer — EARTHING_XFMR

∑ A pass-through leaf, by its fields

It carries primary_voltage but no secondary_voltage, so resolveTransformerModel treats it as a plain leaf: a dead-end shunt with no through-power and no loading %. Its z0_ohm (plus 3 × any neutral_resistance_ohm) is read by groundfault.go as the zero-sequence path, turning an ungrounded bus grounded and bounding SLG current.

∑ Why no subtypes

Zig-zag and grounded-wye-delta present the same connection points: a three-phase line terminal plus a neutral/earth terminal. No terminal or behaviour difference, so the construction is a coded attribute (GROUNDING_TYPE).

A subtype is for differences the network can see: different terminals, different engine behaviour. Everything else is a field.

The control room

Protected, controlled, kept alive in a grid failure. Contained, deliberately not connected.

The enclosure — SUBSTATION_PANEL

Each cubicle in the control building is a SUBSTATION_PANEL, with geometry_type 'none' like BAY. Its meaningful form is a schematic, not a GPS point; CRUD rejects one created with geometry.

panel_type

PROTECTIONCONTROLMETERINGRTU_SCADAAUXILIARY_DCMARSHALLING

One class, six roles, distinguished by a field.

control_mode

LOCALREMOTELOCAL_REMOTE

Whether the bay can be operated from SCADA or only at the panel.

▶ Placing Adenta’s panels

Create one SUBSTATION_PANEL per cubicle: a PROTECTION and a CONTROL panel per transformer bay, a METERING panel, an RTU_SCADA cabinet and an AUXILIARY_DC board. They are geometry-none, so create them as records, not map points, and stamp them on the one-line later (Chapter 7).

∑ Two ‘none’s tell the whole story

geometry_type 'none': the only visual form is a diagram placement, so each class needs an explicit render_context 'diagram' symbology rule or it renders as nothing. network_role 'none': no terminals, no wiring, no trace. They join containment (SUBSTATION_PANEL contains STATION_BATTERY, all one_to_many) and nothing else.

Coded roles, not subtypes. One standard subtype each. Every field is nullable in the DB with requiredness enforced through subtype_field_rules, because the compiler emits NOT NULL for nullable = false, which fails on a populated table when a re-seed adds a field. Nullable-in-DB keeps every re-compile additive.

04

The LVAC layer

The station’s own AC supply, and the answer to “what feeds the charger?”

A HV/MV station makes its own auxiliary supply: a station-service transformer taps the MV bus and steps it down to 400/230 V, feeding an LVAC board that distributes to every auxiliary circuit. A modelling review found the gap: yard ✓, control-room DC ✓, station AC ✗.

AUX_TRANSFORMER

Point junction, one standard subtype, terminals T1 (MV in) and T2 (LV out). Maps to CIM PowerTransformer.

primary_voltagecoded, required · e.g. 11KV
secondary_voltagenumeric · e.g. 400
impedance_pctnameplate %Z
rated_kvarating → loading %
condition_ratingcoded, required

LVAC_BOARD

Point junction, same one-in/one-fan-out shape as LV_BUSBAR: T1 incoming, T2 fan-out. Maps to CIM BusbarSection.

board_refasset tag (nullable)
phasecoded PHASE, required
incoming_rating_aincomer rating
num_waysoutgoing ways
condition_ratingcoded, required
▶ Wiring Adenta’s LVAC

Place an AUX_TRANSFORMER tapping the 11 kV bus and wire its T1 to the bar’s T2. Place the LVAC_BOARD, wire its T1 to the transformer’s T2, then run LV_CONDUCTOR from the board’s T2 to each auxiliary circuit. A downstream trace from the 11 kV bus now reaches the board, and de-energising the 11 kV isolator shows the station’s own supply going dark.

The connectivity graph

Turning placed features into a network a trace can walk.

Rules are directed

MV_CONDUCTOR → POWER_XFMR HV and POWER_XFMR LV → MV_CONDUCTOR are two rules. Direction tells a downstream trace which way current flows, which is why switches also have a Tn → MV_CONDUCTOR rule.

No unique constraint

A rule set is a collection of rows, so seeds guard inserts with WHERE NOT EXISTS, not ON CONFLICT. Re-running never duplicates.

A missing rule is a silent “no”

No is_valid rule, no wire. The rules are the schematic grammar of your network.

Connectivity is not relationships

SUBSTATION contains AUX_TRANSFORMER is a relationship; the station does not conduct to the transformer. AUX_TRANSFORMER T2 → LVAC_BOARD T1 is connectivity; it is a wire. Both can exist between the same pair and neither implies the other.

Cardinality is enforced: api/internal/relationship/cardinality.go caps the destination side, so a transformer has at most one station while a station holds many. A second parent is refused.

The one consumer — CIM export

Turning VoltEdge into an IEC 61968-13 CDPSM model needs nesting, and nesting is containment. SUBSTATION maps to CIM Substation with cim_kind 'container'; the exporter reads the tree to place each piece of equipment inside its Substation and, where present, its Bay.

∑ Three tables drive the export (migration 0034)

cim_profiles (RDF/XML header; default CDPSM_CIM16), cim_class_mappings (which CIM class, and the cim_kind axis), cim_attribute_mappings (e.g. POWER_XFMR.rated_mva scaled to VA). Authored in seed/cim_mapping_example.sql, editable in the admin CIM-mappings screen.

Three guards against silent drops: an admin coverage card listing unmapped classes with a prefilled “Map…” button; a CIM WARNING log with the dropped count; and a per-class CIM preview showing the byte-exact XML for one real feature. The LVAC seed ships its own mappings, which are skipped on a database without migration 0034.

∑ Context-aware geometry and symbology

Diagram rendering uses render_context 'diagram', falling back to 'map'. Geometry-none classes have no map rule to fall back to, so the DC classes carry an explicit diagram rule. ANSI one-line stencils (two-/three-winding transformer, open switch) are the natural follow-up for classes that ship with a plain diagram node, such as POWER_XFMR and the LVAC classes. Each open diagram has its own coordinate space, with a per-diagram status bar showing live cursor X/Y.

▶ Building Adenta’s one-line
  1. On the SUBSTATION, Dana chooses Create diagram → ONE_LINE. A blank canvas opens.
  2. Stamp the 33 kV bus as a bar across the top, the two transformers below, the 11 kV bus as a second bar, and bay breakers as boxes.
  3. Stamp the panels and the DC classes. This is the only view where they are visible at all.
  4. Place the amber aux transformer off the 11 kV bar and the olive LVAC board below it.

The diagram shows the network; it does not define it. Drawing a line between two symbols does not wire them.

08

Making it live

No analysis knows what a substation is. That is the point.

Trace

A downstream trace from the 33 kV incomer walks through the transformer, down the 11 kV bus, out every feeder, and down the station-service tap into the LVAC board with zero special handling. It is gated by two things: device_state OPEN is a barrier, and edges are never the “to” side of a rule. Running it also stamps circuit_ref. Try it on the one-line in Chapter 1.

Load flow — field names are load-bearing API

The engine asks one structural question of every junction: does it carry both primary_voltage and secondary_voltage? It never checks the class name. Toggle fields below to see what the engine would make of a node, using the rules in Appendix C.

Fault study — sink and source

A fault sink

Prospective short-circuit current at the 11 kV bus accumulates source impedance back to the grid through POWER_XFMR’s windings. Transformer impedance dominates; the station is where fault level is set. The aux transformer adds a modest series impedance on its branch.

A fault source

Embedded generation superimposes a second infeed on the Thévenin impedances, so the bus fault level rises. SLG stays grid-only, with EARTHING_XFMR’s z0_ohm setting the zero-sequence path. The clearing device the study names is the upstream bay breaker, a junction it can point at.

Building Adenta end to end

One procedure, then the tables you keep on a second monitor.

The build, in order

  1. The place. Draw the SUBSTATION polygon “Adenta Primary.” Containment root; not connected.
    nodes
  2. The yard, HV to LV. 33 kV bus → incomer and bay breakers → two POWER_XFMR (rated_mva, impedance_pct) → 11 kV bus → feeder breakers → EARTHING_XFMR (z0_ohm).
    nodes
  3. The control room. SUBSTATION_PANELs, the DC layer, plus PROTECTIVE_RELAY / MV_CT / MV_VT.
    nodes
  4. The LVAC tap. AUX_TRANSFORMER (small rated_kva) off the 11 kV bus, LVAC_BOARD below.
    nodes
  5. Wire connectivity in path order. Each hop needs a matching network_rule or the connect is silently refused.
    wires
  6. Establish containment. Equipment under the SUBSTATION; DC devices under their panels.
    views
  7. Draw the one-line. The geometry-none control room’s first visual appearance.
    views
  8. Run it. Trace (stamps circuit_ref), load flow, fault at the 11 kV bus, hand to OMS.
    analyses

Nodes before wires, wires before views, views before analyses.

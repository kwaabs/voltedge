01

What the field tool is

An offline-first app: pull a pack, work anywhere, sync when you’re back.

Before you head out you pull a pack — a self-contained copy of one work area — onto your device. In the field you view the network, edit it, create and delete features, wire connections, take photos, run inspections, raise faults and scan asset tags — all with no network connection. Everything is saved on the device. When you’re back in coverage you sync, and your work goes to the office system.

The golden rule: your edits are never lost. They live on the device until the server confirms it received them — and then the app tells you whether each one was accepted or needs your attention.

You only need a connection at the start (download your work area) and at the end (sync). Everything in between is fully offline. Flip the signal switch and try each action:

03

Your work area: the pack

A snapshot of one bounded area at one moment in time.

  • It’s bounded. You choose the area (a box on the map) and which layers to include. A pack covers that area — not the whole network. For a different area, download a different pack.
  • It’s a snapshot. The pack reflects the network as it was at the moment it was built. If the office changes something afterwards, your pack won’t show it until you download a fresh one. (It matters at sync time — chapters 5 and 6.)
  • It carries only what you’re allowed to see. The extras a pack ships — connectivity links, relationships, the work board, the fault board, asset tags — depend on your account’s permissions. If your pack has no work board or fault board, your account isn’t set up for them; ask your administrator.

05

What happens when you sync — the lifecycle

Two stages — and understanding the difference saves confusion.

  1. Captured — your device hands the edit to the server, which stores it durably. It’s safe on the server but not yet applied to the live network.
  2. Applied — moments later the server processes the edit against the live network. This is where it’s actually accepted — or held back.
APPLIED ✓

Done. The office now has your change.

REJECTED ✗

It couldn’t be applied — a rule failed, or a connection you drew isn’t allowed. Lands in needs attention with a reason.

CONFLICT ⚠

Someone in the office changed the same feature after your pack was built; applying yours would overwrite theirs. Held for a supervisor.

SUPERSEDED –

You edited the same feature twice offline; only your latest is kept. Silent — nothing for you to do.

A dropped connection mid-sync is safe
Whatever was captured stays captured; you just sync again to send the rest. The full lifecycle runs against your own edits in chapter 19 — make a few in the phone first.

07

Getting set up — the Connection screen

Point the app at your server and sign in. The gear icon on the home screen.

  1. fieldsync base URL — your server’s address, e.g. http://10.0.2.2:3706 on an emulator or http://<LAN-ip>:3706 on a real device (your administrator gives you this). Tap Save base URL if you change it.
  2. Enter your Email and Password (the eye icon shows/hides it) and tap Sign in. The button reads Signing in… while it works.

When you’re signed in the screen shows Signed in as …. You stay signed in — you don’t log in every time. Use Sign out only when handing the device to someone else: it keeps the base URL and just drops your login. You never type or see a “token”.

08

Getting your work area — downloading a pack

Pick up a shared pack (instant) or request a new one (draw an area and wait for the build).

On the home screen (VoltEdge Field) packs are in three lists: On this device (ready offline), Shared packs (ready-made areas an administrator pre-built for your team — a plain download, no build wait), and Available from server (packs you asked the server to build). Use whichever fits.

Interactive · 7

VoltEdge Field — home screen

Requesting a new pack

  1. Tap New pack.
  2. Set the Bounding box — easiest is Draw on map: tap two opposite corners of your work area (or Use current view), then Use this box.
  3. Under Feature classes, tick the layers you need. Use the search box and the per-group All / None toggles; the count shows N selected.
  4. Tap Request build. It moves through queued → running → succeeded (then Download) — or failed (tap to see why).
Downloads and syncs need signal; everything in between does not
Download your packs at the depot on wifi before you head out — a pack can be tens of MB.

Shared packs — what’s different

  • It carries the published network only. A shared pack is a clean, team-wide copy, so it has no one else’s draft work — only published features. Your own drafts still come along (below).
  • Your work board comes with it. If your account uses Work & Asset Management, your assigned jobs are added when you pick it up.
  • It shows a Shared label on the device and works exactly like any other pack. A crew can keep one for weeks and sync each week — you only pick up a fresh copy to get the latest published state.

My drafts

Your own drafts — features you’ve started but not published — aren’t part of the published shared base, so they’re delivered separately: a My drafts button on Pack detail lists them; on the map they’re green like an offline-created feature. Tap one to read it, then Edit attributes to keep working — changes sync back the normal way.

Using and managing a pack

Tap a downloaded pack to open Pack detail (summary, feature classes, My drafts if any, Open map). The pack’s ⋮ menu has Open map and Delete (from the device). Use the stop icon to Cancel a build that’s queued or running.

09

Looking at a feature — the detail panel

Open a pack and tap Open map. The phone on the right is that workspace.

From here on, the phone is live — everything you do in it is real to the guide’s own sync engine (chapter 19). The sample pack has three poles, a transformer, two switches and three conductors from the Oakwood feeder. Missions tick off as you go.

Tap a feature to select it. It gets a red outline and opens its detail.

If several features sit on the same spot, a “N features here” sheet lets you pick the right one — so a line hidden under a point stays reachable. (Try tapping POLE-102 — three conductors start there.) The detail panel shows Identity (ID, Class, Status, Subtype, Territory), Attributes (N), Photos, GPS survey quality, Connections (N) and Relationships (N). The neighbour rows under Connections/Relationships are tappable — jump to that feature and walk the network hop by hop.

Phone vs tablet

On a tablet the detail is a 360 px panel beside the map; on a phone it’s a bottom sheet. The action icons across the top of the panel are where you Edit attributes ✎, Edit geometry ⌖, Connect 🔗, Relate ⇄, Delete 🗑 and — for a feature you created offline — Discard ↩.

10

Editing a feature’s attributes

The panel becomes a form; the controls match each field’s type.

  1. Select the feature and tap Edit attributes ✎.
  2. Controls follow the field type: a switch for yes/no, a dropdown for coded fields (pick from the allowed list), a date picker, a number keyboard for integer/decimal, plain text otherwise.
  3. Fields marked with a red * are required for this feature’s subtype — Save is blocked until they’re filled.
  4. Fields under LOCKED AFTER PUBLISH (padlock) can’t change once published — shown read-only for reference.
  5. Tap Save (or Cancel).

The feature now shows a hollow amber ring on the map and an “Edited offline — not yet synced” chip. It stays that way until you sync.

Revert and it disappears
Revert a field to its original value and it drops out of your pending change automatically — undo every change and the pending edit is gone. Try it on POLE-101: change material, save; edit again and put it back.
11

Moving a feature — the geometry editor

Drag to move; the original position is hidden so you only see where it is now.

  1. Select the feature and tap Edit geometry ⌖.
  2. A bar appears: Point — drag the point to move it. Line / polygon — drag any blue vertex; tap a white midpoint to add a vertex; select a vertex and use Delete selected point to remove one (down to the minimum — 2 for a line, 3 for a polygon).
  3. Undo steps back one change; Cancel throws the edit away. Tap Save.

A moved feature shows as a filled amber dot. Like an attribute edit it’s pending until you sync.

Snapping
Turn on Snapping in Map settings and a placed point, dragged vertex or inserted midpoint snaps onto the nearest existing feature vertex — so a new connection lines up exactly with the asset it joins.

Creating a feature — point, line, polygon

Class → draw → Place → form → Create.

  2. Choose the class. Classes you can’t draw are greyed out as … — not supported.
  3. Draw the geometry — a banner guides you.
  4. Tap Place.
  5. Fill the create form: pick a Subtype first (it decides which fields are required), an optional Name, then the attributes.
  6. Tap Create.

13

Deleting a feature

Soft-deleted on the server when you sync — and reversible until then.

  1. Select a packed feature and tap Delete feature 🗑.
  2. Confirm on Delete feature? — “… will be marked deleted and soft-deleted on the server when you sync. You can undo before then.”

It disappears from the map and the panel shows a Marked deleted offline… banner. Changed your mind? Tap Undo delete — on the banner right after deleting, or from the pending sheet later (the ⟳ badge) — and it comes back. A feature you created offline is removed with Discard instead — there’s nothing on the server to delete.

14

Wiring connections and relationships

Both work the same way: start on one feature, then tap the other.

Connections (electrical links)

  1. Select a feature and tap Connect to another feature 🔗. (Hidden for features that aren’t part of the electrical network — try it on a pole.)
  2. If the feature has named terminals, pick one on the Terminal on … sheet (or No specific terminal) — try it on TX-101.
  3. A green banner reads Tap the feature to connect to … — tap the target.

The connection is queued and drawn as a green dashed “marching-ants” line — it reads like current flowing, distinct from a real solid conductor. If the pack carries network rules and the connection isn’t allowed you’ll get Connection not allowed and a reason.

Relationships (non-electrical links, e.g. containment)

  1. Select a feature and tap Relate to another feature ⇄.
  2. A purple banner reads Tap the feature to relate to … — tap the target.
  3. If the pair supports more than one kind, pick it on the Relationship to … sheet.

Drawn as a purple dashed line. A pair no class permits shows Relationship not allowed.

Advisory on the device, re-checked on the server
Connections and relationships you make offline are advisory on the device and re-checked by the server when you sync — so an edge the server ultimately rejects comes back in your “needs attention” list. (The sample pack’s rules are a teaching set: a node connects to a conductor; two nodes can’t connect directly; poles support things rather than connect to them.)

Using GPS and external GNSS receivers

The GPS quality pill, top-left of the map.

Tap the pill to expand the live readout: Fix quality (RTK fixed / RTK float / DGPS / GPS fix / No fix) with a coloured tier — green Excellent/Good, amber Moderate, red Poor — plus Accuracy, Satellites (used / in view), PDOP/HDOP/VDOP, altitude, and Internal or External receiver. Wherever you see Use my location it uses this fix, and the quality at that moment is saved with the feature (GPS survey quality).

16

Taking photos

Detail panel → Photos (N) → Add.

Take a photo with the camera or Choose from gallery. Each photo carries a badge: Not uploaded until it syncs, then Uploaded. Tap a photo to view it full-screen (pinch to zoom). Your offline captures open from the device even with no signal; already-synced photos fetch the full-resolution original when you’re online. Photos sync alongside your other changes (chapter 19). Add one to a feature in the phone, then look at the pending sheet.

17

Inspections, defects and faults

If your pack includes Work & Asset Management or Faults data.

You’ll see Work & assets and Faults entry points (on Pack detail, and as badges on the map) — your offline work board and fault board.

Work & assets — three tabs

  • Work — work orders assigned to you. Open one to Start work, Complete (optional Completion notes), Run inspection or Raise defect.
  • Defects — open defects on your assets. Tap Defect to capture one: pick the asset, fill Severity, Title *, type, description, and attach a photo. Update status (Mark work ordered / resolved / dismissed) on an existing one.
  • Inspections — the active checklists. Open one, pick the asset, answer each item (Pass / Fail, or a value/rating, with an optional note), add Overall notes, tap Record inspection. A failed item that carries a severity automatically raises a defect when you sync.
Interactive · 9

Faults — the Faults screen

The open faults on your assets. Tap Fault to raise one: pick the asset (or No asset — report by location to pin your GPS position), choose a Fault type, fill Title * and the rest, and Save fault. On an existing fault use Update status (investigating / cleared / closed) or Book defect to turn it into a WAM defect.

Reporting without an asset
For a problem on something not in the system yet, choose No asset — report by location. The app pins your current GPS position so the office can find it.

Everything you capture here is saved offline and goes up on the next sync, like a feature edit. A Not synced / Captured offline / Raised offline pill marks what’s still pending.

Scanning an asset tag

Tap Scan asset tag (the ▦ on the map).

Point the camera at the QR or barcode on the asset’s tag — the app reads it and jumps to that feature. Use Torch in the dark, Switch camera if needed, or Enter code manually to type a code like VE-7F3K2Q. Scanning works offline against the pack’s tag index first, then checks online if the pack doesn’t have it. Try each of these codes in the phone’s scanner:

19

Syncing your work

When you’re back in coverage, send your work up.

The map’s top bar shows a sync badge with your pending count (and a red “needs attention” badge if an earlier edit was flagged). Tap it to open the pending sheet. It lists everything waiting — edits, creates, deletes, connections, relationships, WAM/fault captures and photos — each with its own Push this change button, plus a single Sync N changes button at the bottom.

This is the same engine as the phone’s badge. Make a few changes in the phone above (or load samples), choose what the “office” will do, and sync:

Interactive · 10

Pending changes

What will the office do? (scenarios)
  • After syncing you’ll see “Synced 4 changes.” (with “ · 1 needs attention” if flagged). A successful sync means your work is safely captured; the office applies it moments later, and anything it can’t apply comes back to needs attention.
  • A dropped connection mid-sync is safe — re-open and Sync again; already-sent changes won’t double up.
  • You don’t have to sync everything at once — Push this change sends one item.
  • Colours clear only when edits are applied and you pick up a fresh pack — the button in the widget runs that.

01

Foundations: the shared network model

Understand this once and every later chapter is the same engine asking a different question.

Your features become a graph

A study never looks at the map. It looks at the connectivity graph and walks outward from a start feature along power flow, building a tree where every feature hangs off the source by exactly one path. Try it: open the switch, or close a second path.

💡 If a study fails with “not radial (loop/mesh detected)”, run LOOP_CHECK_TRACE to find and open the loop, or switch to the meshed or nodal engine.

What each feature contributes

Source · start node

Nominal + Thévenin

nominal_voltage_v is required. source_r_ohm / source_x_ohm make it sag under load and give a finite fault level; absent means an ideal stiff source. Zero-sequence via source_r0_ohm / source_x0_ohm.

Load · generation

kW + power factor

demand_kw and power_factor give Q = P·tan(acos pf); bad pf → unity. Generation is negative load from dispatch_kw (authoritative even at 0), falling back to rated_kw.

EV charging

Diversified, then capped

effective_kw = min(managed_demand_cap_kw, connected_load_kw × diversity_factor), read from the station’s own roll-up. Diversity defaults to 1.0; no cap if absent.

∑ Three-winding units

A tertiary_voltage turns on the star equivalent: three arms meeting at a common point, from the three measured pair impedances.

ZH = ½(ZHL + ZHT − ZLT)    ZL = ½(ZHL + ZLT − ZHT)    ZT = ½(ZHT + ZLT − ZHL)

Each arm is pre-referred to its own winding’s base. With only HV-LV surveyed, it goes on the L arm and the tertiary is ideal. vector_group (default Dyn) is read only by the ground-fault calc.

Conductors and the voltage-base convention

A conductor’s impedance is resistance_ohm_per_km × length_km, with length from ST_Length(geom::geography) in true metres. The engine scans every field on the edge class for one linked to a material spec, so LV_CONDUCTOR and MV_CONDUCTOR needn’t share a field name. Zero-sequence falls back to 3× positive when unrecorded. The spec’s ampacity_amps rides along so overloads are flagged in the same pass.

∑ Why a sweep, not Newton–Raphson

Kersting’s forward-backward sweep is the standard technique for a radial feeder. Loads are constant-power, so demand doesn’t change between passes; only the voltage the drop is measured against is refined, and three passes over-converge at distribution drop magnitudes. A full AC solve would be needless complexity for a loop-free tree; the meshed and nodal engines exist for when it isn’t one.

Tier 1: closing a loop by circulating power

The engine solves a radial spanning tree with each loop broken open, measures the voltage mismatch at the break, and injects a circulating power to drive it to zero. Below, the LB-Oakwood-3 ring: two legs from TX-Feeder7-014’s LV board to a 60 kW load.

How a ring splits its load

§3.3 · inverse-impedance split

The guide gives no impedance for a 95 mm² leg, so the ratio is yours to set. A thinner leg B means ZB/ZA > 1.

∑ The compensation step
M = V(existing) − V(closer)     dP = −M · VrefRloop + k · Xloop    dQ = k · dP

Each broken loop is two tree paths from their lowest common ancestor. V_ref is the endpoints’ own base, so an LV ring isn’t scaled by an MV nominal. Corrections are accumulated and re-applied Gauss–Seidel style so loops sharing branches reconcile; converged when the largest residual is below 1e-4 V. max_iterations defaults to 25; one independent loop closes in one pass.

Why k = Q/P of the load. Continuity gives one equation in two unknowns, cP·R + cQ·X = −V·M. Circulating at the load’s power factor reproduces the exact 50/50 split two identical legs must carry. The loop-impedance-angle alternative (cQ/cP = X/R) was tried first and gave roughly 75/25, so it was replaced.

⚠️ Meshed load flow never silently energises a normally-open tie. A loop that meets at an open device comes back compensated: false, “loop meets at an open device — left open, not compensated.” A loop of only zero-impedance junctions is also compensated: false; its ends are already one point.

Tier 2: the nodal Y-bus

Gauss–Seidel · default

Linear, monotonic, proven

Flat start 1∠0. PQ: I = conj(S/V), V = (I − ΣYijVj)/Yii. PV holds |V| and solves Q. Slack never updated; isolated buses skipped. Tolerance 1e-9 pu, cap 5000 passes, else flagged not converged.

Newton–Raphson · opt-in

Quadratic, for stiff meshes

"solver": "newton". Polar form, J·Δx = mismatch with blocks H N / M L. Tolerance 1e-9, cap 40, usually a handful. Hand-rolled Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting (solveDenseLinear), no external library. PV limits latch one-way.

Where the limiting impedance comes from

∑ Peak (making) duty · IEC 60909
κ = 1.02 + 0.98 e−3R/X  (1.02 < κ ≤ 2.0)     ip = κ √2 Isym

A stiff, inductive path pushes κ toward 2.0, so the crest approaches 2√2 ≈ 2.83× RMS. This is the closed-form making duty, not a time-domain breaking duty, and it does not change the arc-flash figure. The guide gives the formula but not the Adenta number; the calculator applies it to the path’s own X/R.

∑ Generator infeed by superposition

Loop impedance is Z(g) + Z(f) − 2·Z(lca) on a common base. Precedence: fault_contribution_ka → subtransient_reactance_pct on rated kVA → inverters (solar_pv / battery) at 1.2× rated current → none. A synchronous machine gets no default. Keyed off nameplate rated_kw, not dispatch: a curtailed machine still contributes fully. Exact for disjoint paths, slightly conservative when shared. Three-phase only; SLG stays grid-only.

§4.10 · Fault location — running the study backwards

Everything above is the forward question: if a fault were here, how much current? Fault location (POST /analysis/fault-location, Experimental) is the operational inverse after a real trip: a relay measured this current — where is the fault? It is the "FL" that completes the FLISR chain — FLISR restores service around the fault; fault location tells the crew where to walk.

∑ Measured current → distance

Because the prospective current falls monotonically as impedance from the source grows (§4.5), a measured current Im pins a target impedance |Ztarget| = c·VLN / Im. The engine reuses the fault study's per-node accumulated |Z| and walks each conductor span; wherever the span's source-to-span impedance window brackets Ztarget, the fault is on that span. It interpolates the fraction along it (impedance ≈ linear in length) into a distance from the source and a map point, oriented so fraction 0 is the upstream end. A feeder branches, so the same electrical distance recurs on several laterals — every bracketing span comes back as a ranked candidate, the "the fault is one of these" a real fault-locator shows.

Inputs. start is the feeder source (substation bus / transformer secondary), not the tripped device — the same contract as the fault-current study. Plus measured_current_amps and the nominal voltage. The voltage factor c defaults to 1.0 here (a real measurement back-calculates at the actual pre-fault voltage), not the IEC 1.10 rating maximum the prospective study uses.

▶ Reading it. A ranked candidate list — each with its faulted span, the upstream feature feeding it, the distance from the source and along the span, and a red pin on the map. An empty list carries a note explaining why: the current was above the source ceiling (fault at/near the source, or source impedance understated), or below the feeder's minimum (beyond the modelled network, or a high-impedance/arcing fault). A candidate over an unresolved impedance path is flagged as an optimistic estimate.

▶ From a logged fault. A fault event already records its fault_current_a (fed from the device-operation log / SCADA). On the asset's Faults tab, a fault with a measured current shows a Locate button that hands that current to the Fault Location panel — pick the feeder source and the candidates appear, no re-typing.

⚠️ Three-phase only for now (SLG location — the commonest distribution fault — needs the zero-sequence path inverted, the next increment), confined to the faulted voltage level (upstream of transformers, where |Z| is monotone), and radial from the start like every impedance walk. Experimental: the distance is an estimate that narrows a crew patrol from a section to a span — not a surveyed position.

∑ From fault current to operate time
  1. Path: pathToFault walks tr.parent up from the fault, ordered fault-first.
  2. Through-current: Idevice·Vdevice = Ifault·Vfault; a device on the HV side sees proportionally less.
  3. Operate time: inverse-time TD × curve(I/pickup), no operation at M ≤ 1; instantaneous element → 0 s; definite-time → fixed definite_time_s; fuse / recloser → total-clear band.
  4. Grading: first resolved device is primary; gradePairs checks tbackup − tprimary ≥ CTI, skipping non-operating devices. Top-level coordinated is the AND of every gradeable pair.

Plotting curves are referred to one common_base_v (the fault node’s), so the fault current is a single vertical line each curve crosses at its own operate time.

💡 To fix a failing pair, slow the backup (raise its time dial) rather than speeding the primary, which can break its coordination further down. Run the study for a fault at the end of each protected section. The default 0.3 s CTI is relay-to-relay; set your own for fuse-fuse or fuse-relay.

Fuse-saving scheme check

A recloser protecting a lateral fuse runs a two-curve scheme: on a transient fault its fast curve should trip before the fuse melts (the fuse is saved); on a permanent fault the fuse should totally clear before the slow curve locks the feeder out (the fuse clears). The check (POST /analysis/fuse-saving, Derived) evaluates both for every recloser→fuse lateral below a source.

fuse_saved = tfast(I) < tmin_melt(I)     fuse_clears = ttotal_clear(I) < tslow(I)

For each fuse (MV_FUSE, or a fused MV_SWITCH/LV_SWITCH) it finds the nearest upstream MV_RECLOSER by walking the tree parent chain (nearestUpstreamRecloser), then evaluates both verdicts at the prospective fault current at the fuse — the recloser curve read at that current referred to the recloser's base, the fuse bands at the fuse's base. It also reports max_save_current_amps, the fuse-saving crossover where the fast curve overtakes the fuse minimum-melt — the upper bound of the range the fuse is actually saved over — found by log-spaced sampling and a geometric bisection (fuseSaveCrossover). Results list each pair issues-first (pair_count, fuse_saved_count, fuse_clears_count, issue_count), capped at maxFuseSavingPairs = 60.

∑ The Oakwood lateral under REC-7

At the lateral's ~3.9 kA fault level the recloser's fast curve takes ≈ 0.05 s against a fuse minimum-melt of ≈ 0.012 s → fuse_saved: false (the fuse blows on the first fast shot), and the crossover max_save_current_amps lands near 1.1 kA (below that current the fast shot would save it). The slow curve (≈ 0.46 s) sits well above the fuse total-clear (≈ 0.023 s) → fuse_clears: true. The read: run REC-7 fuse-blowing (slow-only) unless the fault current at the tee is below ~1.1 kA — the same conclusion the Switching & Protection guide reaches by hand (§7.9), now computed over every lateral at once.

⚠️ The check evaluates the scheme at one fault current per fuse (the bolted fault at the fuse) plus the crossover — it is not a shot-by-shot sequence simulation and does not model the fuse's cumulative heating from earlier recloser shots. Read fuse_saved: false as "the fuse blows on the first fast shot at this fault level", and max_save_current_amps as the current below which the scheme holds.

∑ The terms, and why there’s no iteration
R(Tc) = R201000(1 + α(Tc − 20))    qr = πDεσ((Tc+273.15)4 − (Ta+273.15)4)    qs = γΦD
Re = D ρf Vμf   qc1 = Kangle(1.01 + 1.35Re0.52)kfΔT   qc2 = Kangle·0.754Re0.6kfΔT   qcn = 0.0205√ρf D0.75ΔT1.25

Air properties are held constant near a ~40 °C film at sea level (ρf = 1.029, μf = 1.9×10⁻⁵, kf = 0.028), so qc and qr depend only on temperatures and the current comes out in closed form. Convection is the greatest of the two forced forms and natural convection, so low wind falls through to the natural floor with no special case. The guide names the two Reynolds forms without spelling them out; the standard IEEE 738 forms and Kangle = 1.194 − cos φ + 0.194 cos 2φ + 0.368 sin 2φ used here reproduce its 339 A and 258 A exactly. Net cooling ≤ 0 reports 0 A.

⚠️ Worst case = hot ambient + near-zero wind + full sun. Always supply a solar figure for a daytime rating; omitted solar assumes a clear night and flatters the result. This is steady-state only, not a short-time emergency rating.

Back-feed capacity — the limiting element and its headroom

The overloaded list answers a binary question. A dispatcher wants the margin: how close is the tightest element, which one, and how much more can it take? backfeedLimit scans the swept donor for the single most-loaded rated element — a conductor against its ampacity, a transformer against its kVA — from the same loading_pct load flow computes. That is limiting_element / limiting_kind (ampacity or transformer); its spare capacity to 100 % follows from one identity:

limiting_headroom_kva = S · 100 − LL   (S = √(kW² + kvar²) at that element, L = its loading %)

A negative figure means the transfer already pushes it past its rating. The response also carries base_worst_loading_pct — the donor's worst loading before any transfer — so each option reads against a "before" picture: Feeder 9 here sits at ≈ 62 % to start, so TIE-79-A takes its trunk cable to 84 % (+72 kVA headroom) while TIE-79-B drives the same cable to 108 % (−58 kVA over). The limiting_element naming that trunk cable is exactly the asset to reinforce if the bigger transfer must be made available.

⚠️ A planning-level margin at the limiting element, not a promise that a given extra load fits anywhere — a transfer loads the donor at specific points, and the headroom is the slack at the one element that binds first. Shares the contingency tier (Derived).
